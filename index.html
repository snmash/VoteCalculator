How it works

Your number is how many eligible citizens there are in your county for every person who actually voted: eligible citizens ÷ votes cast = 100 ÷ turnout %. If only half of eligible citizens vote, each ballot cast speaks for two people.

Data

  • Votes cast, presidential (2024): total votes for U.S. President in the November 2024 general election, by county, from official state records compiled by the MIT Election Data and Science Lab.
  • Votes cast, midterm (2022): the highest vote total for any statewide race (U.S. Senate, governor, other statewide offices and ballot questions) or for U.S. House summed across districts, by county. This is the standard proxy for ballots cast when no single race appears on every ballot. Built from MIT Election Data and Science Lab precinct-level returns. Indiana (where many counties didn’t report precinct data), Phillips County, AR and Power County, ID use county-level certified totals instead.
  • Eligible citizens: citizen voting-age population (CVAP), 2020–2024 American Community Survey 5-year estimates, U.S. Census Bureau special tabulation (released January 2026).

Adjustments

  • Connecticut: election results are reported for the 8 historical counties, while Census now uses 9 planning regions. Town-level CVAP is summed back to the historical counties.
  • Alaska: results are reported by state house district, which doesn’t line up with boroughs, so Alaska is shown as one statewide figure.
  • Kansas City, MO votes (reported separately by its election board) are included in Jackson County. Kalawao County, HI is combined with Maui County, where its votes are counted.

Caveats

  • Turnout is slightly understated. Ballots that skipped the top race are not counted.
  • One population estimate for both years. The 2020–2024 survey estimate, centered on 2022, is the denominator for both elections.
  • Small counties are noisy. Census survey estimates for counties with a few thousand residents carry wide margins of error. Results above 100% are capped at 100%.
  • Some counties include residents who can’t vote there. CVAP counts incarcerated people (ineligible in most states), military personnel registered in their home states, and students registered elsewhere. Counties with large prisons, bases or colleges can show artificially low turnout and a high number.
  • Midterms are different. Turnout in midterm years, such as 2026, typically runs well below presidential years. Nationally it was about 46% in 2022 versus 64% in 2024, which is why the site defaults to the midterm view.
